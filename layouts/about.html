Default bill of materials
  1. Python and Django, PostgreSQL.
  2. HTMX and Alpine.js over a full front-end framework, until the interface actually needs one.
  3. Tailwind, compiled with the standalone CLI. No Node in the deploy path unless a sidecar needs it.
  4. NestJS, React and FastAPI where a client's estate already speaks TypeScript or the job is AI extraction.
  5. AWS in CDK for platforms; one tidy VM for everything that does not need more.
  6. GitHub for source, Actions for CI, Sentry for the 2 a.m. page.

  1. 1.1

    Operators first

    We run a coffee business and use our own software behind the counter. That habit of living with the consequences shapes every build: fewer clever abstractions, more thought about what happens when the internet drops mid-shift.

  2. 1.2

    Small on purpose

    You talk to the person writing the code. Decisions are made in the conversation, not relayed through an account manager three days later.

  3. 1.3

    Boring technology

    Django and PostgreSQL have been unfashionable and reliable for fifteen years. We pick tools your next developer can hire for, and we write down why.

  4. 1.4

    Your code, your accounts

    Source lives in your repository from the first commit. Infrastructure lives in your cloud account. If we part ways, you keep everything and it keeps running.

  5. 1.5

    Straight answers

    If a brief is a bad idea, or a PWA will do the job of the native app you asked for, or a spreadsheet is honestly fine for now, we say so before you spend money.

  6. 1.6

    Shipped beats perfect

    Weekly releases to a staging URL from week one. Real users on real data as early as it is safe. Polish follows evidence.
